# Peer Review Report

## Manuscript Information
Title: Mitochondria-localised ZNFX1 functions as a dsRNA sensor to initiate antiviral responses through MAVS
Manuscript ID: Nature Cell Biology 21:1346–1356 (2019), DOI 10.1038/s41556-019-0416-0
Review Date: 2026-09-16
Review Round: Round 1

## Reviewer Information
Reviewer Role: Peer Reviewer 1 (Methodology)
Reviewer Identity: Quantitative experimental biologist with formal biostatistics training and routine methods/statistics review experience for wet-lab immunology and virology papers.
Review Focus: I will evaluate experimental design, controls, replication structure, statistical reporting, and reproducibility. I will not judge domain novelty or journal fit except where methods limitations directly affect evidentiary strength.

## Scoring Plan
I pre-commit to evaluate the manuscript using the following methodology/statistics criteria before reading the manuscript or source data:

1. Experimental design and causal logic: assess whether each central mechanistic claim is supported by appropriately matched perturbation/rescue, epistasis, localization, interaction, and infection experiments, with controls adequate to distinguish direct effects from indirect antiviral-state or viability effects.
2. Controls: check for non-targeting RNAi/sgRNA controls, rescue or multiple independent knockdowns where RNAi is used, positive and negative infection/stimulation controls, isotype/secondary-only flow controls where applicable, loading controls for immunoblots, fractionation-purity controls for mitochondrial/cytosolic/nuclear claims, and co-IP specificity controls.
3. Replication and sampling: extract stated n values from legends and Methods; distinguish biological from technical replicates where possible; flag ambiguous n, underpowered animal/survival experiments, or pseudo-replication.
4. Statistical methods: check whether the reported tests match the design (two-group versus multi-group, paired versus unpaired, repeated-measures/time-course, survival analysis), whether multiple comparisons are addressed where needed, and whether exact P values, tails, variance summaries, and error-bar definitions are sufficiently reported.
5. Source-data verification: open every deposited XLSX source-data file, map each analyzable panel to the relevant legend/statistical claim, recompute every feasible test from the deposited replicate values using the authors' stated or inferable test where possible, and compare against printed/reported P values and n. I will record successes as well as discrepancies with file/sheet/cell anchors.
6. Reproducibility reporting: assess whether Methods provide enough detail for qRT-PCR normalization, flow-cytometry gating/quantification, immunoblot quantification, infection titres/MOI/timing, animal randomization/blinding, endpoint criteria, exclusion rules, and software/statistical parameters.

Pre-committed fatal triggers forcing a Reject recommendation from this seat: (a) deposited source data cannot support recomputation for a substantial fraction of central quantitative claims because replicate values are absent or uninterpretable; (b) recomputed statistics reveal widespread or decision-bearing non-reproduction that changes significance of core claims; (c) essential negative/positive controls are absent for the core ZNFX1→dsRNA→MAVS causal chain; (d) survival/animal or infection conclusions rest on clearly inappropriate statistical tests or unreported n such that the result is uninterpretable; or (e) internal inconsistencies in n, P values, or source-data values are extensive enough to undermine trust in the quantitative record.
