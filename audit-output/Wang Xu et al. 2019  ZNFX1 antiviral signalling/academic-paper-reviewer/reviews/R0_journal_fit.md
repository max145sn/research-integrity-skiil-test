# Peer Review Report

## Manuscript Information

**Title:** Mitochondria-localised ZNFX1 functions as a dsRNA sensor to initiate antiviral responses through MAVS

**Review Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Review Round:** Round 1

## Reviewer Information

**Reviewer Role:** Journal-Fit Reviewer (handling-editor seat)  
**Reviewer Identity:** Senior handling editor at *Nature Cell Biology* with a cell-biology / innate-immunity portfolio.  
**Review Focus:** I assess venue fit, conceptual advance, claim-evidence alignment, paper architecture, figure/legend quality, and claim hygiene. I do not attempt a full statistical audit or domain-literature adjudication, except where a point is decision-bearing for editorial fit.

## Scoring Plan

Before reading the manuscript, I will judge this submission against five NCB-facing criteria:

1. **Conceptual advance and venue fit.** A manuscript meets the NCB bar if it identifies a genuinely new cell-biological principle or mechanism, not only another antiviral factor. For this topic, the advance must connect nucleic-acid sensing to organelle localization and MAVS signalling in a way that would interest a broad cell-biology / innate-immunity readership.
2. **Headline claim alignment.** The title, abstract and closing paragraph must use claim strength that matches the evidence. Terms such as "sensor", "direct", "dsRNA", "immediate", "specific", "independent" and "therapeutic target" require appropriate mechanistic support, not only correlated antiviral phenotypes.
3. **Evidence sufficiency at the journal-fit level.** I will not re-score statistics in detail, but the main story must include orthogonal evidence across loss/gain of function, localization, interaction, ligand association, pathway placement and organismal relevance. A single-cell-line or single-virus story would fall below the venue threshold.
4. **Narrative and figure architecture.** The manuscript should present a coherent sequence from discovery to mechanism to physiological relevance. Main figures should be interpretable without excessive cross-hunting, and legends should clearly state biological material, perturbation, readout, replication and scope.
5. **Claim hygiene and repairability.** I will distinguish defects requiring new decisive experiments from those repairable by rewriting. Rewriting alone is sufficient only when the underlying evidence supports a narrower claim.

Pre-committed fatal triggers that would force **Reject** from this seat are: (i) no credible evidence that ZNFX1 affects antiviral IFN responses; (ii) no credible evidence placing ZNFX1 at mitochondria/MAVS despite the title; (iii) no credible evidence that ZNFX1 associates with viral RNA or dsRNA-like ligands despite calling it a dsRNA sensor; (iv) the claimed RLR independence is contradicted by the presented pathway data; or (v) the manuscript is so poorly structured that the central claim cannot be evaluated.

## Overall Assessment

**Recommendation:** Major Revision  
**Confidence Score:** 4 — handling-editor fit/readability judgement, not full statistical audit

**Summary Assessment:**  
This is a strong and venue-plausible cell-biology/innate-immunity manuscript. The central storyline is attractive: a comparatively underexplored SF1 helicase is induced by IFN, localizes to mitochondria, associates with viral RNA/dsRNA-like ligands, interacts with MAVS, contributes to IFN induction, and protects cells and mice from RNA-virus challenge. That breadth of evidence gives the paper a real conceptual advance for *Nature Cell Biology*, especially because it links antiviral sensing to mitochondrial MAVS architecture rather than simply cataloguing another ISG. I would not reject from this seat: none of my pre-committed fatal triggers fired. However, I would not accept without substantial revision because the headline language is consistently sharper than the evidence. In particular, "dsRNA sensor", "immediately recognizes", "specific" restriction of RNA viruses, and the early ZNFX1-to-RLR priming loop are presented as settled mechanisms, whereas the data more conservatively support ZNFX1 as a mitochondria-associated antiviral RNA-binding/MAVS-interacting factor that promotes early IFN responses in the tested systems. These are likely repairable through targeted mechanistic clarification and claim-tempering, but they are decision-bearing for an NCB-level Article.

## Strengths

**S1. Strong conceptual opening in an underexplored receptor class.**  
The manuscript frames a clear gap: SF2 helicases are well represented in antiviral sensing, whereas SF1 helicases are much less characterized. Identifying ZNFX1 as a candidate SF1 antiviral factor gives the work a credible novelty hook for NCB readers.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "helicase SF1 is rarely characterized" (Introduction, manuscript_extracted.txt:25-27)

**S2. Orthogonal progression from discovery to in vivo relevance.**  
The story moves from expression/screening to knockdown/knockout/rescue, several RNA viruses, IFN readouts, and mouse infection. This breadth makes the claim more compelling than a single overexpression phenotype.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "generated Znfx1-/- C57BL/6J mice" (Results, manuscript_extracted.txt:196-198)

**S3. Mechanistic cell-biology axis is plausibly developed.**  
The mitochondrial localization, MAVS interaction, truncation mapping and pathway-placement experiments create a coherent organelle-signalling mechanism, which is the feature that most supports NCB fit rather than a narrower immunology-specialist venue.  
**Evidence Anchor**: figure: Fig. 6 panels a-k, mitochondrial localization, protease/alkali extraction, MAVS co-immunoprecipitation and truncation mapping

**S4. The central title elements have substantial, not merely cosmetic, evidence.**  
Although I recommend tempering the wording, the manuscript does present evidence for ZNFX1 expression during viral challenge, antiviral activity, viral RNA/poly(I:C) association, mitochondrial enrichment and MAVS dependence.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "ZNFX1 performs its antiviral roles without depending on RLRs" (Results, manuscript_extracted.txt:2121-2122)

**S5. Reporting package is comparatively complete for editorial assessment.**  
The manuscript includes source-data availability, GEO deposition for RNA-seq, reporting summary information, cell-line authentication/mycoplasma statements, and uncropped blot/source-data material.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "Source data for Figs. 1-7" (Data availability, manuscript_extracted.txt:2860-2863)

## Weaknesses

**W1. The headline "dsRNA sensor" claim is stronger than the shown ligand-mechanism resolution.**  
**Problem:** The title and abstract present ZNFX1 as a dsRNA sensor, but the manuscript's mechanistic evidence primarily shows association with viral RNA, binding to poly(I:C), preference for HMW poly(I:C), and domain requirements for poly(I:C) pulldown. That supports an antiviral RNA-binding sensor-like role, but it does not fully establish what endogenous viral dsRNA species are sensed at mitochondria or whether dsRNA recognition is the decisive physiological trigger.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "mitochondrial-localised dsRNA sensor" (Abstract, manuscript_extracted.txt:79-80); text: "preferable binding of ZNFX1 proteins to HMW poly(I:C)" (Results, manuscript_extracted.txt:945-947)  
**Why it matters:** This phrase is the main title-level mechanistic claim. At NCB, "sensor" and "dsRNA" signal a direct molecular recognition mechanism, not only RNA association plus downstream IFN phenotypes.  
**Suggestion:** Either temper to "viral RNA-binding MAVS-associated antiviral factor" / "dsRNA-like ligand-binding factor" or add decisive experiments using purified ZNFX1 domains and defined RNA substrates, endogenous viral dsRNA enrichment/depletion, and loss-of-binding mutants that uncouple RNA binding from MAVS interaction and antiviral signalling.  
**Severity:** Major  
**Confidence:** 4 — based on claim-evidence alignment, not a biochemical re-analysis

**W2. The "immediate" early-sensor / RLR-priming loop is framed as more settled than the temporal evidence warrants.**  
**Problem:** The abstract says ZNFX1 "immediately recognizes" viral RNA and forms a positive loop of RLR signalling, while the Discussion infers that ZNFX1 is "probably an early sensor" that primes RIG-I/MDA5. The manuscript shows earlier expression/localization and RLR-independent activity, but the causal temporal sequence from ZNFX1 sensing to early RLR priming is not nailed down as a mechanism.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "immediately recognizes viral RNA" (Abstract, manuscript_extracted.txt:81-82); absence: Results/Discussion — expected time-resolved epistasis distinguishing early ZNFX1 priming from later RLR amplification; checked Fig. 4, Fig. 7, Discussion and Extended Data Fig. 7  
**Why it matters:** The early-sensor model is a major conceptual advance over "another MAVS-linked antiviral factor". If the temporal hierarchy is inferential, the abstract and model should not present it as established.  
**Suggestion:** Add synchronized infection time courses with early ligand binding, MAVS activation, IFN induction, and RIG-I/MDA5 induction in WT, Znfx1-/-, RLR-deficient and rescue cells; alternatively, revise the abstract/model to state that the data are consistent with an early priming role.  
**Severity:** Major  
**Confidence:** 4 — editorial mechanism-claim judgement; detailed kinetics left to methods/statistics reviewers

**W3. The RNA-virus specificity claim is too broad for the tested pathogen scope.**  
**Problem:** The abstract states that ZNFX1 "specifically restricts the replication of RNA viruses". The main evidence spans VSV, EMCV and H1N1 as RNA viruses, with HSV-1 as the principal DNA-virus contrast. This is suggestive but not enough to support a broad specificity claim across RNA-virus classes or to exclude narrower virus-, cell- or route-specific effects.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "specifically restricts the replication of RNA viruses" (Abstract, manuscript_extracted.txt:80-81); text: "VSV, EMCV and H1N1 but not HSV-1" (Results, manuscript_extracted.txt:201-205)  
**Why it matters:** "Specifically" is a scope claim. Overstating scope can mislead readers about pan-RNA-virus generality and about how broadly the proposed sensor operates.  
**Suggestion:** Rephrase to "restricts the tested RNA viruses" or "contributes to antiviral responses against multiple RNA viruses" unless additional virus families/cell types or a mechanistic reason for RNA-virus specificity are supplied.  
**Severity:** Major  
**Confidence:** 4 — based on venue-level scope hygiene, not exhaustive virology literature review

**W4. Therapeutic language is unsupported by the presented study.**  
**Problem:** The manuscript moves from mechanistic antiviral signalling to therapeutic targeting without pharmacologic, translational, safety or disease-model data.  
**Evidence Anchor**: text: "provides a target for antiviral therapy" (Introduction, manuscript_extracted.txt:59-61); text: "may serve as a strategy for early therapeutic intervention" (Discussion, manuscript_extracted.txt:2199-2202)  
**Why it matters:** For a mechanistic NCB Article, speculative therapeutic language is acceptable only if clearly labelled as distant implication. Here it appears in high-salience opening and closing positions.  
**Suggestion:** Remove or soften these statements to "may inform future studies of antiviral intervention" and keep the take-home message on cell-biological mechanism.  
**Severity:** Minor  
**Confidence:** 5 — directly within claim-hygiene/editorial-scope remit

**W5. Main-figure density makes the argument less publication-grade than the underlying story.**  
**Problem:** Several main figures are highly compressed multi-assay composites, especially the RNA-binding and pathway-placement figures. The legends are detailed, but readers must integrate many experimental modalities within single figure spreads.  
**Evidence Anchor**: figure: Fig. 5 panels a-l, RNA immunoprecipitation, genome mapping, ligand competition, truncation and oligomerization sequence  
**Why it matters:** The venue bar is not only evidentiary breadth but communicative precision. Dense composites can obscure which evidence supports each claim element, especially the distinction between viral RNA association, dsRNA-like binding, MAVS interaction and signalling output.  
**Suggestion:** Restructure the figure sequence or add clearer subheadings/schematic logic within figures: ligand association, binding specificity, domain requirement, mitochondrial placement and pathway epistasis should each have an explicit claim-to-panel map.  
**Severity:** Minor  
**Confidence:** 4 — based on handling-editor presentation assessment

## Detailed Comments

**Title & Abstract.**  
The title is memorable and broadly appropriate for NCB, but it is too categorical on "dsRNA sensor" unless the authors either add mechanistic ligand-discrimination evidence or temper the wording. The abstract effectively explains why SF1 helicases are interesting, but "immediately recognizes", "specifically restricts", and the "positive loop" phrasing should be softened unless new temporal/biochemical data are added. The strongest supported abstract-level formulation is that ZNFX1 is an IFN-stimulated, mitochondria-associated viral RNA/poly(I:C)-binding factor that promotes MAVS-dependent IFN responses independently of RLRs in the tested systems.

**Introduction.**  
The Introduction does a good job opening the SF2-to-SF1 gap and positioning MAVS/STING adaptors for a broad cell-biology readership. The final introductory sentence, however, reaches prematurely toward antiviral therapy. The introduction would be stronger if it ended on the mechanistic unknown rather than a translational promise.

**Literature/Framework.**  
The framework is adequate for this seat: it identifies known RLR and helicase precedents and makes clear why SF1 is underexplored. I defer to the domain-literature reviewer on whether key contemporaneous sensors are missing. For journal fit, the important point is that the manuscript explains why a mitochondrial SF1 helicase sensor would be conceptually distinct.

**Methodology.**  
At the editorial level, the methods are broad and generally appropriate: RNAi, CRISPR knockout, rescue/overexpression, multiple viruses, BMDMs/MEFs/A549/293T cells, mouse infection, immunoprecipitation, RIP-seq, pulldowns, localization and reporting-summary disclosures. I have not performed a statistical audit. The main methodology issue relevant to my remit is that the mechanistic labels in the title/abstract require more discriminating ligand and time-order evidence than the present methods clearly provide.

**Results.**  
The Results are logically ordered: discovery/expression, IFN-stimulated status, antiviral function, IFN signalling, viral RNA binding, mitochondrial/MAVS mechanism and RLR independence. This structure fits an Article. The weaknesses are mostly claim calibration: the results support a substantial ZNFX1 antiviral signalling role, but "dsRNA sensor", "immediate" and broad RNA-virus specificity are stronger than the most conservative reading.

**Discussion.**  
The Discussion is useful in contrasting ZNFX1 with RIG-I/MDA5 and in articulating the early-sensor model. The prose should more clearly distinguish demonstrated findings from inference. Phrases such as "probably an early sensor" are appropriately cautious, but the abstract and graphical model are less cautious than that Discussion sentence.

**Conclusion.**  
The closing paragraph captures the intended advance but should be tightened. I would keep the conclusion centred on a mitochondria-associated ZNFX1-MAVS axis in early antiviral IFN induction and remove or substantially qualify the therapeutic-intervention claim.

**References.**  
The cited framework appears adequate for editorial fit, with appropriate references to RLRs, MAVS, STING, ISGs, DExD/H helicases, ARM-containing mitochondrial proteins and C. elegans ZNFX1 biology. I did not audit completeness against the specialist literature.

## Questions for Authors

1. What specific endogenous viral RNA species do the authors believe ZNFX1 recognizes in infected cells, and what evidence distinguishes dsRNA sensing from broader viral RNA association?
2. Can the authors provide a time-resolved causal chain showing ZNFX1-dependent RNA binding/MAVS activation before RIG-I/MDA5 amplification during early infection?
3. What is the intended scope of "RNA-virus specificity" beyond VSV, EMCV and H1N1, and should the claim be restricted to the tested viruses?
4. Are there separation-of-function ZNFX1 mutants that retain MAVS localization/interaction but lose RNA binding, or vice versa, to connect ligand recognition to signalling output?

## Minor Issues

- The manuscript contains a typographical inconsistency in the closing paragraph: "ZFNX1" should be "ZNFX1".
- "promotor" in the Fig. 1 legend should be "promoter".
- Some figure legends use very long, multi-clause sentences; readability would improve with shorter clauses or semicolon-separated experimental descriptions.
- Ensure consistent spelling of "localised/localized" according to journal style across title, headings and extended-data captions.
