## Executive Summary

The paper identifies a novel class of exon–intron fusion transcripts ("Rab22a-NeoF1–6") generated by chromosomal translocations that juxtapose the first 38 codons of RAB22A with inverted intronic/UTR sequence from chromosome 20, and shows these fusion proteins drive lung metastasis in osteosarcoma cell lines, patient tissue, and orthotopic/tail-vein mouse models. Mechanistically, the Rab22a1–38 moiety is shown to bind constitutively to the non-canonical Rho-GEF SmgGDS-607, releasing GTP-bound RhoA and activating a pro-migratory/invasive/adhesive phenotype; two point mutations (R4D/K7D and R4E/K7E) that abolish SmgGDS-607 binding rescue the phenotype in both directions (loss- and gain-of-function), which is strong internal consistency evidence for the proposed mechanism. The authors then translate this into a cell-penetrating iRGD-conjugated peptide that competitively disrupts the Rab22a-NeoF1–SmgGDS-607 interaction and suppresses metastasis in vivo, framing a therapeutic angle.

Overall, the mechanistic chain (fusion discovery → phenotype → interactome → binding-site mapping by charge-reversal mutagenesis → RhoA activation → peptide inhibition → in vivo rescue) is unusually complete and internally consistent, and the loss-of-function/gain-of-function/rescue triad used throughout is a methodological strength. The principal limitations are (1) a very small clinical cohort (2/37 metastatic, 0/60 non-metastatic samples harbor the six screened fusions), which is too small to support a generalizable prevalence or prognostic claim even though the paper is appropriately cautious about this in the Discussion; (2) heavy reliance on stable overexpression/knockdown systems and a single primary antibody-validation pathway for the endogenous protein, with relatively sparse orthogonal validation (e.g., no independent mass spectrometry replicate reported, no CRISPR knock-in of the native fusion locus); and (3) statistical reporting that is minimally described (Student's t-test throughout, no correction for multiple comparisons across the many pairwise contrasts, sample sizes generally n=3 or n=6 without power justification). None of these undermine the qualitative discovery, but they temper how strongly the quantitative/prevalence claims should be read.

## Summary of Contributions

1. Discovery of a novel class of exon–intron (rather than exon–exon) gene fusions, Rab22a-NeoF1–6, generated by chromothripsis/chromoplexy between chromosome 20 and chromosome 8 in osteosarcoma, using fusion-callers (TopHat-fusion, SQUID) that specifically retain intron-spanning reads.
2. Functional demonstration that the shared Rab22a1–38 fusion moiety is necessary and sufficient to drive lung metastasis (not proliferation) in vitro and in orthotopic/tail-vein mouse models, and that analogous Rab22a1–38 fusions (RAB22A-MYO9B, RAB22A-TEF) occur and are functional in breast and lung cancer cell lines.
3. Mechanistic elucidation that Rab22a1–38 constitutively binds a negatively charged surface of SmgGDS-607 via Arg4/Lys7, displacing/activating RhoA–GTP, and that this axis is necessary for the metastatic phenotype (validated by RhoA knockdown/knockout rescue and DD/EE charge-reversal mutants).
4. Development and validation of a monoclonal antibody (RAD5-8) and humanized derivative (hRAD5-8-v1-R5) specific to the neo-epitope at the fusion breakpoint, enabling endogenous fusion-protein detection, plus a nanoparticle-delivered siRNA and an iRGD-conjugated competitive peptide that each suppress Rab22a-NeoF1-driven metastasis in vivo, proposing both diagnostic and therapeutic translational tools.

## Strengths

### S1: Rigorous, bidirectional (gain-of-function / loss-of-function / rescue) experimental logic
Throughout Figs. 1–8, essentially every mechanistic claim is tested in both directions: overexpression of Rab22a-NeoF1/Rab22a1–38 increases migration/invasion/metastasis (Fig. 1d–g), while siRNA/shRNA knockdown of the endogenous fusion decreases it and is rescued by re-expression (Fig. 3c,d and Extended Data Fig. 3c,d). The same logic is repeated for RhoA (Fig. 4c–j) and for the SmgGDS-607 binding interface via charge-reversal mutants (Fig. 6f–k). This internal-consistency structure is a genuine strength that substantially raises confidence in the causal chain beyond what any single experiment could support.

### S2: Use of intron-retaining fusion-calling methodology to find a previously inaccessible class of fusions
The authors explicitly note (Introduction) that standard fusion-finding pipelines filter out intron-associated structural variants, and they deliberately chose TopHat-fusion and SQUID because these tools preserve exon–intron and intron–intron fusion reads. This is a methodologically well-motivated choice that plausibly explains why RAB22A-NeoF1–6 were not previously reported, and the cross-validation of six independently found isoforms strengthens confidence that this is a real signal rather than an alignment artifact.

### S3: Cross-cancer generalizability check
Rather than stopping at osteosarcoma, the authors mined the cBioPortal (TCGA) and CCLE datasets for RAB22A fusions in other tumor types, found candidate fusions in breast (BT-474) and lung (HCC2935) cancer lines, and functionally validated that knockdown of these fusions reduces migration/invasion and (for BT-474) reduces lung metastasis in a tail-vein model (Fig. 2e–m). This substantially broadens the claimed generality of the Rab22a1–38 mechanism beyond a single tumor type, which is an important strength for the paper's overall significance claim.

### S4: Translational follow-through to a therapeutic candidate
The paper does not stop at mechanism; it maps the SmgGDS-607 binding determinants down to two residues (Arg4/Lys7), separately maps the RhoA-activation determinant to a different residue (Lys18), and uses this structure–function map to design a short competitive peptide, which is then made cell-penetrating with iRGD and shown to block metastasis in vivo (Figs. 7–8). Distinguishing "binding" from "activation" residues (peptide(WT) binds SmgGDS-607 but is *not* sufficient to activate RhoA; Lys18 mutation does not affect binding but abolishes activation) is a genuinely careful piece of structure–function dissection that adds credibility to the proposed mechanism.

## Weaknesses

### W1: Small and likely underpowered clinical cohort for the prevalence/prognosis claim
Only 2 of 37 (5.4%) metastatic and 0 of 60 non-metastatic osteosarcoma samples were positive for the six screened fusion transcripts (Results, "Identification..." section; Supplementary Table 2). With only two positive cases, the reported median-survival comparison (9.5 vs 19 months) cannot be considered statistically robust, and the paper does not report a formal statistical test or confidence interval for this comparison — likely because n=2 makes this infeasible. The Discussion appropriately hedges ("Similar functions of Rab22a truncations may occur with a frequency higher than the 5.4%... as we only tested six fusion transcripts"), which is good scientific practice, but the survival-time contrast is still presented in the main figures/tables in a way that a reader could over-interpret as a validated prognostic biomarker. **Suggested fix**: explicitly caveat the survival comparison as descriptive/hypothesis-generating (n=2 vs n=35) rather than juxtaposing it near the quantitative prevalence statistic without a matching statistical caveat, and/or screen a larger, ideally multi-institution cohort with all six (or additional) fusion isoforms plus untargeted fusion detection before drawing prognostic conclusions.

### W2: Statistical methodology is minimally specified and does not address multiple comparisons
The Methods state only that "P values were obtained by two-tailed Student's t-test" and that mouse survival used Kaplan–Meier/log-rank tests (Statistics and reproducibility, p. ~28 of the PDF extraction). Given the very large number of pairwise comparisons performed across Figs. 1–8 (dozens of P values reported per multi-panel figure, e.g., Fig. 4c–j alone reports 16+ P values), there is no stated correction for multiple testing, no mention of normality/variance-equality checks that would justify a parametric t-test over multiple biological replicates of n=3, and no explicit statement of exact vs. approximate degrees of freedom or whether comparisons were pre-registered/planned vs. post hoc. This is a common limitation in mechanistic cell-biology papers of this era and does not invalidate the qualitative direction of effects, but it does mean the exact P values should be read as indicative rather than rigorously corrected significance levels. **Suggested fix**: report the statistical test per comparison family with a multiple-comparisons correction (e.g., Holm–Bonferroni or FDR) where many related contrasts are shown in one panel, and state replicate numbers (biological vs. technical) more explicitly panel-by-panel.

### W3: Endogenous-protein validation relies on a narrow set of tools with limited orthogonal confirmation
Endogenous Rab22a-NeoF1 detection depends almost entirely on one custom monoclonal antibody (RAD5-8) and its humanized derivative, validated primarily by comparing RAB22A-NeoF1-positive vs. -negative tissue (Fig. 3b) and cell lines (Fig. 3c). The paper does not report an independent orthogonal method for endogenous protein confirmation (e.g., mass-spectrometry detection of the fusion-specific peptide directly from tumor lysate, or independent antibody raised against a different fusion epitope) beyond RNA-level RT–PCR/FISH. Given that antibody specificity for a short (15-mer) neo-epitope can be difficult to fully rule out for cross-reactivity, an additional orthogonal validation (e.g., targeted MS/MS of the breakpoint peptide in a positive tissue) would strengthen the endogenous-protein claims materially, especially since the FISH signal is explicitly described as "relatively low" (p. ~5 of extraction) because the fusion-positive population is a small subset of the tumor tissue.

### W4: Data/code availability statement leaves several derived analyses unreproducible from the paper alone
The Data Availability statement provides the SRA accession for raw RNA-seq/WGS (SRP181860) and points to TCGA/cBioPortal/COSMIC for the public reanalyses, which is good practice. However, it also states "All other data supporting the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request" for a substantial fraction of primary data (e.g., full TAP–MS interactome dataset beyond what's summarized in Supplementary Table 5, exact fusion-calling intermediate files/parameters beyond the one representative TopHat command line quoted in Methods, source cell-line stocks). No code repository (e.g., GitHub/Zenodo) is referenced for the bioinformatics pipeline beyond citing the tool names and one example command line, which limits independent reproducibility of the fusion-detection step specifically, even though the raw sequencing data itself is deposited.

## Questions for the Authors

1. Given only 2/37 fusion-positive metastatic samples, was any additional untargeted (RNA-seq-based, rather than the 6 pre-specified RT–PCR targets) fusion screen performed on the full 97-sample cohort, or on an independent validation cohort, to estimate the true population frequency of Rab22a1–38-containing fusions more precisely?
2. For the RAD5-8/hRAD5-8-v1-R5 antibody, was cross-reactivity against wild-type Rab22a or unrelated proteins with similar epitopes formally excluded (e.g., by peptide-competition or knockout-cell-line controls), beyond the RAB22A-NeoF1-positive vs. -negative tissue comparison shown in Fig. 3b?
3. Was a multiple-comparison correction considered for the large panel-wide sets of P values (e.g., Fig. 4, Fig. 6), and if not applied, can the authors clarify whether comparisons were pre-specified per panel or exploratory?
4. Is bioinformatics pipeline code (the exact SQUID/TopHat-fusion parameter scripts and downstream filtering logic used to call and prioritize the six Rab22a-NeoF fusions from among all detected candidates) available in a public repository, to allow independent reprocessing of the deposited SRA data?

## Minor Issues

- The PDF/figure-legend text interleaving in the extracted manuscript (a two-column layout artifact, not a manuscript defect) made some passages harder to parse sequentially during review; this is a review-process note, not a criticism of the published PDF itself.
- Figure legends (e.g., Fig. 1, Fig. 3, Fig. 4) are very information-dense, packing many sub-panel statistical annotations into compact legends; a reader unfamiliar with the panel layout may find it easier to consult the Reporting Summary/Methods concurrently.
- The manuscript inconsistently capitalizes "Rab22a" (protein) vs. "RAB22A" (gene) — this is standard nomenclature convention and not an error, but worth noting it requires attention when tracing specific claims between gene- and protein-level statements.

## Literature Positioning

*(Limitation: live web literature search was unavailable this session — see disclosure at top — so this section is based on the paper's own reference list and general domain knowledge rather than a fresh independent search.)*

The paper positions itself against the well-established landscape of exon–exon structural-variant fusion discovery in cancer (citing prior work on osteosarcoma's characteristic chromothripsis/chromoplexy and comparing its chromosome 20–chromosome 8 rearrangement to EWSR1-ETS fusions in Ewing sarcoma) and against the specific gap that conventional fusion-calling pipelines discard intron-spanning reads. The choice to use TopHat-fusion and SQUID specifically because they retain such reads is well justified and clearly cited. SmgGDS (RAP1GDS1) as a RhoA/RhoC-specific non-canonical GEF is grounded in prior literature the authors cite (ref. 32), and the iRGD tumor-homing/cell-penetrating peptide strategy is grounded in previously published iRGD literature (refs. 36–37) rather than novel peptide-delivery chemistry. Overall the related-work framing appears adequate and specific citations are provided at each key methodological choice point; a fully independent literature search would still be advisable before treating this positioning as verified, given the search-tool limitation disclosed above.

## Recommendations

**Overall Assessment**: Accept (as a published, peer-reviewed *Nature Cell Biology* article, this reflects a retrospective assessment of the manuscript's quality rather than a live acceptance decision)

**Confidence**: Medium — High confidence in the mechanistic/experimental-design assessment (based on a complete read of the main text, Methods, and Reporting Summary); medium confidence in the literature-positioning assessment specifically, because live citation verification was not possible in this session.

**Contribution Level**: Significant — the discovery of a new, previously overlooked class of intron-retaining driver fusions, combined with a full mechanistic chain to a validated peptide therapeutic candidate, represents a substantial and well-supported advance, tempered by the clinical-cohort-size and statistical-reporting limitations noted above.

### Actionable Suggestions for Improvement
1. Expand and independently validate the clinical fusion-prevalence screen (larger cohort, untargeted fusion detection) before the survival-time contrast is used to support any prognostic-biomarker framing in follow-up work.
2. Apply and report multiple-comparison-corrected statistics for the many within-figure pairwise contrasts, and state the biological-vs-technical-replicate structure per panel explicitly in a supplementary statistics table.
3. Deposit the fusion-calling analysis code/parameters (not just the tool names and one example command) in a public repository (e.g., GitHub/Zenodo) alongside the SRA-deposited raw data, and add at least one orthogonal (e.g., mass-spectrometry-based) confirmation of the endogenous fusion protein beyond antibody detection.
